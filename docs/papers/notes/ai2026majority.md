## Summary

Argues standard majority voting treats all answers equally and so ignores latent heterogeneity and correlation across models, and designs two training-free aggregators, Optimal Weight and Inverse Surprising Popularity, using first- and second-order information. Theoretical analysis shows these provably mitigate majority voting's limitations under mild assumptions, validated on synthetic data, UltraFeedback, MMLU and a real-world healthcare setting. Gives the paper a principled, citable alternative to naive pooling and a formal handle on why correlation-aware weighting beats vote counting.
