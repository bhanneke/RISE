## Summary

Recruits 43 expert researchers to execute randomly assigned research ideas, either expert-written or LLM-generated, each spending over 100 hours and writing a 4-page paper, then has the projects blind-reviewed. Review scores of LLM-generated ideas fall significantly more than expert-written ideas on novelty, excitement, effectiveness and overall (p < 0.05), closing and in several metrics reversing the ranking observed at the ideation stage. The strongest existing warning that apparent novelty of LLM-generated research output is not the same as epistemic quality - directly relevant both to why this paper measures justification soundness and traceability rather than surface novelty, and to designing a research-forecasting task whose outcome is verifiable.
