## Summary

Latent class analysis of 3,785 PhD students from Nature's Graduate Survey 2025 recovers four attitudinal profiles toward AI across five research tasks. The dominant profile is an explicit 'division of labour': AI accepted for tracking and summarising literature, resisted for writing, data analysis and experimental design, i.e. exactly the tasks tied to intellectual contribution, authorship and responsibility. Gives empirical shape to the human-AI division-of-labour question rather than asserting it, and draws out implications for governance, doctoral training, disclosure and research evaluation.
