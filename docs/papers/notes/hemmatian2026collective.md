## Summary

Synthesises network science, collective cognition and multi-agent systems, reviewing how task environments, group topologies, agent-level processes and incentive structures shape collective outcomes in human-only and AI-only networks and asking which findings survive the move to hybrid groups. It explicitly discusses how established exploration-exploitation and efficiency-redundancy trade-offs may operate differently once agents are LLMs, and closes on implications for organizational design and governance. Useful as a bridging citation between the organizational-design canon the paper builds on and the network-science literature, and as an existing statement that classical topology results need revision for LLM agents.
