## Summary

A formal theory of when multi-agent organization amplifies or destroys weak signal, built from exactly the primitives this paper cares about: a per-agent compute-performance exponent, a communication fidelity curve, an effective shared-error correlation rho, and a context window that imposes fan-in limits and thereby makes hierarchy necessary. For binary tasks with majority aggregation they prove a sharp phase transition for deep b-ary trees under correlated inputs and lossy communication, and derive an organization exponent s such that outperforming the best single agent under the same budget occurs exactly when s exceeds the single-agent scaling exponent. Closed-form risks for star, chain and tree organizations make correlation- and communication-induced performance floors explicit. Effectively a Sah-Stiglitz-style organizational-design theorem restated for LLM agent stacks, and the strongest formal complement to the paper's argument.
