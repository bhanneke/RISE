## Summary

Extends the DeGroot social-learning model with an AI aggregator that trains on population beliefs and feeds synthesized signals back to agents, defining a learning gap as the deviation of long-run beliefs from the efficient benchmark. The main result is a threshold in updating speed - if the aggregator updates too quickly, no positive-measure set of training weights robustly improves learning, whereas such weights exist when updating is slow enough - and the architectural comparison shows local aggregators trained on proximate or topic-specific data robustly improve learning in all environments, so replacing specialized local aggregators with one global aggregator worsens learning in at least one dimension of the state. For a paper claiming that aggregation architecture determines epistemic quality, this is the closest formal economics result: global versus local aggregation is a hierarchy-versus-polyarchy claim in disguise, from an authoritative team, and it is missing from the knowledge base.
