## Summary

Explicitly compares independent aggregation against deliberative consensus on 1,189 resolved prediction-market questions, holding the evidence layer constant so reasoning is isolated from retrieval quality. Independent aggregation with confidence-weighted voting is best at 83.43%, while deliberative consensus degrades to about 76%, below every single-model baseline, attributed to error propagation where confidently wrong models flip correct ones. It further reports cross-model error correlations of 0.529-0.689 and argues these place a fundamental limit below the theoretical Condorcet ceiling. The paper's independent-versus-deliberative contrast and its confidence-weighting aggregation rule, on a forecasting task, in a single study.
