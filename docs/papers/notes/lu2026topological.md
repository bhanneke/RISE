## Summary

The strongest available large-scale empirical statement of the paper's core claim that structure, not individual model capability, is the binding constraint on collective epistemic output. Using a platform of 1.6 million registered agents (174,458 active), plus 1,040 controlled simulations across 22 frontier models from ten vendors and human comparison networks, the authors document 'topological collapse': hub dominance degrades higher-order group interactions into star-shaped broadcast patterns, killing the cohesive structure that discontinuous social contagion requires. Critically, the topological indicators are invariant across models under a fixed protocol while behavioural outcomes diverge, which is exactly the identification argument the paper needs to attribute epistemic quality to architecture rather than to the backbone. Formalised via a Hyperedge Irreducibility Score and an analytical topology amplification factor.
